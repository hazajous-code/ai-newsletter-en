EDITOR'S NOTE

Where AI is good, someone's expertise got there first

A radiologist earns in one hour what a data worker earns in a week. What AI companies are buying isn't working hours; it's an expert's judgment that "this answer is right, and this one is wrong."

Where that judgment piles up, AI improves fast. The standards of the doctor who graded medical explanations and the lawyer who corrected legal memos become the model's skill.

The change that began with text has passed through images and now stands before video. At every stage, there's one thing AI hasn't bought yet: judgment about why our customers hesitate and what they want.

What AI learned was someone's judgment. Judgment about our customers is still ours.

※ This is the editorial view of this issue, not a proven fact.

01 · Key trend

Doctors and lawyers teaching AI on the side — up to $400 an hour

What changed

Data work for AI training1 was once seen as low-paid clickwork. Now doctors, lawyers and engineers teach AI as a side job. Bloomberg reported on Oct 16 that this kind of "expert data work" is growing quickly.

※ Rates as advertised. Mercor says its workers average $110 an hour but hasn't disclosed what share of tasks pays that rate.

Evidence

Experts do three things. They write problems and reference answers based on cases or problems they've handled, grade the AI's answers, and correct its mistakes. A legal expert, for example, checks whether the citations in an AI-drafted memo support its points; a doctor judges whether an AI's medical explanation is accurate.

The field is growing. Mercor was valued at $10 billion in October 2025, when its expert network passed 30,000 people. UK platform Prolific says experts make up 30% of its participants. In Korea, Crowdworks sells question-and-answer datasets3 of more than 3.5 million items across 16 fields.

Marketing impact

If AI suddenly got good at something, there's a good chance experts' grading rubrics in that field went into the model. Marketing is no exception: the "generally good answer" for judging copy or interpreting campaign results increasingly comes from AI.

What can't be bought or sold is the record of our own customers. The evidence behind judgments only we can make — customer inquiries, reviews, service conversations — isn't in any model.

Limits

Top rates are advertised figures, not actual earnings. Even the radiologist takes on at most two projects a year. Antonio Casilli of the Polytechnic Institute of Paris points out that most of the work is still done by low-paid workers. The work is unstable — jobs can dry up or accounts be suddenly deactivated — and Mercor has faced at least seven class actions after a data breach. MIT's Danielle Li warns that experts' knowledge stays in the model after companies stop paying.

Verdict

Write it down. Document the standards your team uses when it fixes AI output (brand tone, banned phrases, words customers dislike). Those standards are your own grading rubric.

02 · Outlook

Text → image → video — is video the last frontier?

What changed

There's a saying in the industry: "Text has fallen, images are falling, and video is what's left." Some go further and predict that once video falls, we'll experience a real singularity. Here's where each stage stands now.

StageWhere it is nowEvidence
TextAn everyday work toolConversational AI has become a mass channel — enough for ChatGPT ads to expand to 89 countries (Oct 21)
ImageBecoming a standard featureCanva released its first in-house image model, Craft, to all users (Oct 22). As AI images became hard to tell from real ones, Etsy and Pennsylvania made rules for AI product images (Oct 22)
VideoStill expensive and shortOpenAI closed its Sora video app in April and ended the API4 on Sept 24, reportedly to redirect computing power to coding and enterprise. Google's Gemini Omni Flash tops out at 10 seconds and 720p5 in Flow; Kuaishou's Kling 3.0, 15 seconds per shot
Evidence

Video is seen as the last frontier because it captures how the world moves. Yann LeCun, who left Meta to found AMI Labs, says "LLMs6 are too limiting" and argues for world models7 that predict the physical world rather than text. Google DeepMind8 presented Genie 3, which generates explorable 3D worlds in real time, as "a new frontier for world models." OpenAI also said Sora continues as world-model research after the shutdown.

There's a counterview. Anthropic's Dario Amodei has suggested that, building on text-based models, "a country of geniuses in a datacenter" could arrive as early as 2026.

Marketing impact

Product images and banners have already become something anyone can make, which is why rules are following. Video still faces limits on length, resolution and cost, so brand video production won't be upended right away. But what happened to images over one or two years could repeat in video.

Limits

"A singularity once video falls" is a forecast, not a prediction backed by accumulated evidence. Whether world models are a better path than language models is debated within the industry. Video models' length and resolution figures vary by model and plan, and change quickly.

Verdict

Prepare. Apply the sequence images went through (falling production costs → quality controversies → labeling rules) to video, and set a one-line standard for your brand's use and disclosure of AI video now.

03 · This week

News marketers should catch this week

  • ChatGPT ads go to 89 countries (Oct 21) OpenAI opened ads in 26 more countries and said more than 100,000 advertisers had run campaigns over about eight months (cumulative). Ads are matched to conversations through "context hints9" that describe "what it's for, who it's for and when it's useful," not keywords. They're shown only to adult users on the Free and Go plans and don't appear in sensitive conversations such as health or politics. Korea has had a pilot since June 19.
  • Two sets of AI product-image rules in one day (Oct 22) Etsy now requires one unedited photo of the real item per listing and bans AI-generated patterns and recipes. Pennsylvania signed a law (SB 806) requiring disclosure on AI ads that mislead consumers (in force 60 days after signing). The test isn't "did you use AI" but "does it mislead customers about what they'll receive."
  • Anthropic's proactive agent10, Claude Duet (Oct 21) It checks connected apps every few hours, flags things to handle and sends a morning briefing, asking for approval before sending messages or paying. It's a waitlisted research preview for US paid subscribers. If an agent screens a customer's inbox first, our messages pass through that filter too (our view). Anthropic makes the AI used to write this newsletter.

04 · Platform updates

Operational changes to check this week

Platform update alert

  • ChatGPT ads: Expanded to 89 countries (Oct 21). In Korea, a pilot for adult users on the Free and Go plans has run since June 19. Buying is through technology partners or Ads Manager self-serve; country-specific minimum budgets and billing currencies weren't in the announcement.
  • Pennsylvania SB 806: Takes effect 60 days after signing. If you use AI-made product images, video or audio in campaigns targeting the US, check the disclosure wording requirements.
  • Etsy seller rules: One unedited photo of the real item per listing; no AI-generated patterns or recipes. If you sell on Etsy, review the photos in your existing listings.
  • Korea FTC virtual-person labeling (in force since June 1, background): If an AI virtual person appears in an ad, a "virtual person" label is required, and even with the label, presenting it as a real experience can count as unfair advertising.

GLOSSARY

Glossary

  1. AI training data Problems, answers and evaluations used to teach an AI model. When experts write the problems and do the grading, the model improves quickly in that field. ↩
  2. Mercor A US platform that connects experts with AI companies' training-data work. ↩
  3. Dataset A collection of material gathered in the same format to train AI. ↩
  4. API A connection point for programs to exchange functions. Developers use it to plug AI into their own services. ↩
  5. 720p · resolution A measure of video sharpness. 720p is HD, 1080p is Full HD, and 4K is about four times that. ↩
  6. LLM (large language model) AI trained on vast amounts of text to understand and write like a person. The engine behind services like ChatGPT and Claude. ↩
  7. World model AI built to predict how the world moves by learning from video and the physical world, not just text. ↩
  8. Google DeepMind Google's AI research lab, the maker of AlphaGo and the Gemini models. ↩
  9. Context hint In ChatGPT ads, a sentence describing who the product is useful for and when. Used to match ads to conversation situations instead of keywords. ↩
  10. AI agent Unlike a chatbot that only answers questions, an AI that takes a goal, breaks it into steps, uses apps and actually carries out the work. ↩
