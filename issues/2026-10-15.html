EDITOR'S NOTE

When anyone can build the same features, what gets you chosen?

In Anti-Intellectualism in American Life, published in 1963 and awarded the Pulitzer Prize the following year (1964), Richard Hofstadter distinguished between intelligence and intellect.

IntelligenceA practical mind that moves toward a given goal. It grasps, manipulates, reorders and adjusts. Questions that don't serve the goal get set aside.
IntellectThe critical, creative and contemplative side of the mind. It examines, ponders, wonders, theorizes, criticizes and imagines.

AI agents read binaries3 and reproduce their features, re-save Photoshop PSD4 files byte for byte, and port 20-year-old games to the browser. The territory of "intelligence" — repeating tasks that have a defined right answer — is being copied fast.

In our view, what's hard to copy is the question. Why is this customer hesitating? What did they come in expecting? Does our product answer that expectation? Asking about and reading customer intent, then feeding it back into products and messages, is the "intellect" that remains a marketer's job.

Features get copied. Reading customer intent and acting on it does not.

※ This is the editorial view of this issue, not a technically proven limit.

01 · Key trend

Cloning features got easy — differentiation moves to understanding customers

Key trend: reverse engineering × AI

What changed

Rebuilding a program's features from the finished program alone, without its source code, used to take skilled people years. It is now work that AI agents repeat on their own.

100K+GitHub stars5 for REA about a week after release (reports range from 115K to 134K)
600–700BTokens6 used over three months by the MW2 (2009) restoration project (developer's estimate)
134/135PSD test files that PhotoCraft re-saved byte for byte
Evidence

REA (Reverse Engineer Anything), an open source7 tool released on Oct 7, connects coding agents such as Claude Code, Codex and Cursor to reverse engineering tools such as Ghidra8 and IDA. The game community's "matching decompilation9" judges success by whether the rebuilt result is identical to the original down to the byte, so agents can iterate for hours without a human.

Marketing impact

Products and digital assets that stood out on features alone (apps, web tools, campaign microsites) will be caught up with faster. Pricing leverage with the marketing SaaS15 we use may shift too. The axis of competition moves from "what can we build" to "whose problem, exactly, are we solving."

Limits

Opening and re-saving a file is not the same as real-world work quality. ArtCraft's promise of "100% of features within a month" slipped to months; redistributing original assets is likely infringement; and whether code written by a model trained on the original counts as a "clean-room reimplementation16" is uncertain.

Verdict

Prepare. This isn't a reason to adopt tools right away, but it is time to write down what gets our brand chosen beyond features — customer understanding, data, relationships.

Background research: how customer search intent is changing

In an Apr 27 blog post, Microsoft Advertising argued that customer intent now forms in AI conversations such as Copilot and then carries into search for comparison, confirmation and purchase. From its own search data, it said three- and four-word queries leading to clicks on AI Answers17 rose significantly year over year, while the share of single-word queries fell by 6% (a share of queries, not search volume). The IAB and Talk Shoppe study it cites (October 2025, 600 US adults aged 18–64) found that more than 80% of respondents who shop with AI rated AI most effective at the product research and comparison stage. That is not a figure for AI usage among all shoppers.

※ Published by an ad platform itself; most figures are re-cited from outside research.

When customers stop saying "sneakers" and start saying "sneakers that won't hurt my flat feet on a long walk to work," the brands that can read that sentence have the edge.

02 · Security

About $25 per AI scan — cheaper than a pair of sneakers or a tire

What changed

As AI agents automate everything from finding vulnerabilities18 to breaking in, the human time and skill that made up most of an attack's cost are dropping out. The AI usage fee to automatically scan one target is now less than the price of a pair of sneakers or a tire.

$25.46Average AI usage cost across 101 completed AI scans (low $3.13 · high $79.31). Derived by a security firm from the attacker's records — not the full cost of one successful breach
27+Companies breached Sept 10–15 (to varying degrees), spanning online retail, hotels, airlines, industrial distribution and more, including companies outside the US
600KCredit card records taken from two companies

※ Figures from security firm Gambit's Sept 22 report. The sneaker and tire comparison is ours.

Evidence

According to Gambit, one attacker used three open source AI tools — Strix (vulnerability discovery), Cairn (automated intrusion) and Hermes (overall orchestration) — connected to multiple AI models via OpenRouter19. OpenRouter billing records show $7,005 in AI usage over four weeks; adding roughly double the call volume over the following three weeks, Gambit estimated total AI usage at $12,000–18,000 (an estimate that excludes labor, infrastructure and other costs). Break-ins typically took less than a day, often just hours, and victims included a Fortune 500 hotel company and a major US airline. The skimmer20 code that stole card data was hidden in places like the jQuery21 library and Google tags22.

IncidentWhat happenedTakeaway
Series of Korean bank breachesLate Sept to early Oct. CrowdStrike's analysis found the AI intrusion tool ARTEX, published on GitHub, used together with an LLM23. A bank loan inquiry service and an employee mobile work system were breachedCrowdStrike rated the hacker's skill as "very low" — they left attack logs on a server visible from outside
At least 27 companies, mostly online retailGambit report. US, New Zealand, Japan and elsewhere. 105 attacks with three AI tools, 600K card records stolen, skimmers installed on many sitesAI usage cost to scan one target fell to tens of dollars
Anthropic threat reportA Chinese-speaking group attacked about 50 organizations in education, finance, manufacturing and government. The two operators were identified as university students"Multi-stage attacks that once took several skilled experts were run by one person"
Marketing impact

The assumption that "we're too small to be worth targeting" collapses. As attack costs fall, small online stores, campaign sites and contest entry pages become profitable targets. The checkout step, where the customer has decided to buy, is exactly where skimmers strike — and the code hides in the tags and scripts marketing teams add. However well you read customer intent, trust disappears at once if you can't protect that customer's data.

Limits

Gambit's figures are the firm's own analysis and have not been independently verified. The $25 is the average AI usage cost of 101 completed scans, not the cost of one successful breach or the whole campaign. Neither the Korean bank cases nor the Anthropic report involved new attack techniques; most relied on leaked accounts, exposed services and phishing24.

Verdict

Check now. Start with the list of tags and third-party scripts on your website and campaign pages, who has tag management access, and whether finished campaign pages are still live.

03 · Agents

Meta Muse — an agent that chooses, asks and pays for the customer

What changed

Meta launched Muse, a consumer AI agent, in the US on Sept 8. It isn't a chatbot that only talks: it takes a customer's goal and handles product search, bookings, sending emails and even payment. People use it conversationally in the Muse app (iOS and Android), on the web (muse.ai) and in WhatsApp, and connect other apps such as Instagram if they choose. Most features are free, with a separate paid plan.

1.8MUS and Canada iOS downloads in the 12 days after launch (ChatGPT: 1.3M in the 12 days after its 2023 iOS app launch — each measured at its own launch)
642KUS mobile daily active users at the same point after launch (ChatGPT: 231K). Muse was on iOS and Android, ChatGPT on iOS only; comparing iOS alone, Muse had 359K
2.8MGlobal installs in the same period · No. 1 overall on the US App Store

※ Apptopia estimates (reported by TechCrunch, Sept 21). Not official Meta figures.

Evidence

The field grew quickly in the month or so after launch.

How it works has also been disclosed. Muse runs inside a dedicated per-user virtual machine, "Muse Secure VM," and a separate process called "Sentinel" monitors every outbound action. Meta says Muse doesn't access passwords or payment details directly and always asks for user approval before sending messages or buying. Meta has said it won't put ads inside Muse, and Mark Zuckerberg has mentioned a long-term model of taking a small fee on transactions.

Marketing impact

Customer purchase intent is flowing into conversations with agents instead of search boxes or ads. With no ad slots inside Muse, choices happen in a place where brands can't buy exposure. For an agent to pick our product, product information, prices, stock and reviews must be accurate and machine-readable, and our product must answer the situation the customer describes, such as "something to wear to a wedding in Barcelona in November." With the 9/29 update connecting ad accounts, the state of your campaign naming and account hygiene becomes how the agent understands your brand.

Limits

Download and user numbers are third-party estimates, and the launch is US-only (small-business features are listed as US and Canada). There are reports of Muse appearing in parts of Asia such as India, but no launch has been announced for Thailand or Korea. WIRED reported that Muse generated explicit sexual conversations and that Meta defended this as "lawful adult behavior"; The Verge raised privacy concerns because the Mac app accesses messages, calendars and notes. Official connectors don't include Thai platforms such as LINE.

Verdict

Watch and prepare. It hasn't launched in Korea or Thailand, but cleaning up the product feeds, product detail pages and review data that agents read will pay off with other shopping agents, not just Muse.

04 · AI slop

Can make ≠ should ship — the slop backlash and the return of the human hand

What changed

Backlash against mass-produced, low-quality AI content — so-called "AI slop28" — is turning into concrete costs in advertising and publishing. On the other side, brands are promoting the very fact that people made their work.

Evidence

Cases came one after another this week alone.

CaseWhat happenedResponse
Fanta Halloween ad (reported 10/8)Coca-Cola's Fanta released "Haunted Universe," an AI-generated Halloween ad made with AI studio GRAiL, planned as an annual seriesSocial posts saying "never buying Fanta again"; criticism that it "cheapens the brand." No official company statement was reported
Fake AI books on Amazon (10/10)An 89-page book set in Comic Sans that nearly copied the title of an Australian ABC journalist's book was found; knockoffs from the same template exist for other authors' booksAmazon removed the book the day after being asked; dozens of similar books remained on sale. The Australian Society of Authors called for platform accountability
Major US publishers (10/10)WIRED report: about 20 staff at HarperCollins, Simon & Schuster and Hachette (three of the "Big Five") said anonymously that back-cover copy and marketing materials are made with ChatGPT and Claude, and cover art with AI, without author consentHachette: AI is used "only for operational tasks" and not supported for creative use. Simon & Schuster: only certain vetted tools are used and use is not mandatory. HarperCollins did not respond. In July, a Macmillan imprint cancelled a roughly $2M deal for a novel suspected of being AI-generated

The other side. On Oct 13, Apple's "Welcome Home" campaign released a film of twin sisters in Sydney hand-embroidering the 1977 rainbow logo. It chose to show people making something stitch by stitch rather than a finished image.

Data. An Ipsos and Syracuse University study cited by Fortune (3,000 US consumers, 20 ads) found that human-made ads scored about 14% higher than AI-generated ads on predicted short-term sales impact and about 17% higher on long-term brand health. A study in the Journal of Business Research reported that disclosing AI use makes consumers feel less effort went in, lowering their willingness to engage with the brand and their perception of quality.

Marketing impact

What makes slop repellent is less "they used AI" than the impression that "nobody thought about us." Work that fails to read the care and context customers expect from a brand looks like slop. Even with the same AI, the outcome changes when people decide who to speak to and what to say, check facts and brand standards, and are open about AI use. Showing "how people made it," as Apple did, can itself be a differentiating message.

Limits

The Ipsos and Syracuse study is a prediction based on 20 ads, not actual sales results. The publisher case rests on anonymous testimony, and the publishers said they limit how AI is used. The penalty for disclosing AI use varies by study, and some experts say the effect is larger the more handmade the product itself is.

Verdict

Decide now. Set review standards and a disclosure principle for AI-made work (when to say AI was used) at the team level, even in a single line. The most expensive moment is when "we used it quietly" comes out from outside.

05 · Platform updates

Operational changes to check this week

Platform update alert

  • Meta ads: Testing AI-generated landing pages shown in place of the website for some ads (described as under 5% of impressions). If your brand guidelines are strict, check the "test AI-generated features" setting.
  • WhatsApp (from Oct 1): Service replies and utility templates inside the 24-hour window are also charged per message. The 72-hour free window after click-to-WhatsApp ads remains (per partner guidance citing Meta documentation).
  • Meta Marketing API: v24 ended Oct 6. From Oct 27, some v26 feature removals are set to apply to all versions (details per partner guidance; check Meta's official changelog). Check the versions your automation tools and dashboards use.

GLOSSARY

Glossary

  1. AI agent Unlike a chatbot that only answers questions, an AI that takes a goal, breaks it into steps, uses apps and actually carries out the work. ↩
  2. Reverse engineering Taking a finished product apart to work out how it was designed and how it works. In software, analyzing a finished program to see how it was built without its blueprint (source code). ↩
  3. Binary A program file of 0s and 1s that a computer runs directly (.exe and so on). People can't read it. If source code is the recipe, the binary is the finished dish. ↩
  4. PSD Photoshop's own file format. It holds editing information such as layers, so other programs have struggled to open it accurately. ↩
  5. GitHub · stars GitHub is the world's largest site where developers post and share code. A "star" is a kind of "like." ↩
  6. Token The unit AI uses to process text (roughly a word fragment). AI is billed by tokens, so "using many tokens" means running a lot of AI — and spending on it. ↩
  7. Open source Software whose blueprint (source code) is published so anyone can read and modify it. Terms such as commercial use vary by license. ↩
  8. Decompiler (Ghidra, IDA, etc.) A tool that turns a binary back into code people can read. Ghidra (a free tool released by the US National Security Agency) and IDA are well known. ↩
  9. Matching decompilation An approach that counts as success only when the rebuilt code compiles into a program identical to the original down to every 0 and 1. The clear right answer makes it easy for AI to iterate without people. ↩
  10. WebAssembly Technology that lets heavy programs like games run fast inside a web browser — no installation, just a link. ↩
  11. DMCA The US Digital Millennium Copyright Act. It's the basis for rights holders requesting removal of infringing content (a "DMCA takedown"). ↩
  12. Port (porting) A version of a program made for one device (e.g., Xbox) that has been adapted to run on others (PCs, phones, etc.). ↩
  13. Disc image A file copying the entire contents of an original game disc. It assumes only owners of the original can make one. ↩
  14. Ribbon The menu and button area at the top of Word and Excel, grouped by tabs. ↩
  15. SaaS Software as a Service: software you use on the web for a subscription instead of installing it (e.g., Canva, HubSpot). ↩
  16. Clean-room reimplementation Building something from scratch by people who have never seen the original code, using only its public features and behavior. The traditional way to avoid copyright infringement. ↩
  17. AI answers A feature that shows an AI summary of several web pages above search results, giving the answer directly. ↩
  18. Vulnerability A security gap in a program or website. Attackers get in through it to steal data or plant code. ↩
  19. OpenRouter A broker service that lets you pick and use AI models from many companies through one account. ↩
  20. Skimmer Code secretly planted on a checkout page to steal the card details customers type in. Originally a card-copying device attached to ATMs. ↩
  21. jQuery A widely used shared bundle of JavaScript code for making web pages interactive. Many sites use it as is. ↩
  22. Google tag Tracking code added to websites to measure Google Analytics and ad performance. Usually installed and managed by marketing teams. ↩
  23. LLM (large language model) AI trained on vast amounts of text to understand and write like a person. The engine behind services like ChatGPT and Claude. ↩
  24. Phishing A scam that uses emails or texts impersonating banks or companies to get people to enter passwords or personal data. ↩
  25. Frontier model A top-tier AI model with the highest performance at the time. ↩
  26. API A connection point for programs to exchange functions. "API pricing" is what companies and developers pay to plug AI into their own services. ↩
  27. Open-weight model A model whose trained values (weights) are published so it can be downloaded and run on your own device. Training data and code are often not published, and terms of use vary by license. ↩
  28. AI slop "Slop" originally means pig feed or food scraps. A mocking term for low-quality AI content churned out carelessly in bulk. ↩
