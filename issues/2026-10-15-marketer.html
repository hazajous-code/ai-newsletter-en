01 · Case study

Walmart Sparky — say your goal, and the cart fills up

Real caseDisclosed on 2026 earnings callsResults: company executives' statements · not independently verified

Walmart added the AI shopping assistant Sparky to its app in June 2025 and has disclosed results on every earnings call in 2026. It is a case of turning the moment a customer describes what they're trying to do, instead of typing a search term, into a sale.

Company / timing / sourcesWalmart (US). Launched June 2025. Executives disclosed figures at earnings calls and investor events in Feb (Q4), Apr–Jun (Q1) and Aug (Q2) 2026. Reported by Talk Business (7/9) and Benzinga (8/24); Walmart launch announcement (June 2025)
Problem to solveTyping a product name into a search box and scanning results made it hard to handle goal-driven shopping in one go, like "high-protein meals for this week" or "clothes for the weather where I'm traveling."
Actual scopeAn "Ask Sparky" button in the app. Answers product questions, compares, summarizes reviews, recommends, then adds to cart. In 2026: Spanish support, in-store use, automatic reordering of frequent items. Also works inside ChatGPT since March.
Customer intent revealedCustomers ask in terms of goals and situations. E.g., "Plan a healthy high-protein week of meals" → suggests recipes and meal kits and adds them in one step, recognizing items already bought so they aren't bought again. Q1 units sold quadrupled year over year, driven by repeat purchases of essentials and groceries.
How it works · human judgmentA conversational assistant layered on existing AI features (search, review summaries, product descriptions, comparisons). Testing "Sponsored Prompts2" shown as ads inside the assistant. How products are recommended and where humans review was not disclosed.
ResultsUsed by about half of app users; users' average order value3 about 35% higher (executive remarks, Feb–Apr). Weekly active users up more than 110% quarter over quarter; GMV4 through Sparky up 150% (Q1). Usage up 70% year over year; users spend 40% more per order (Q2, CEO John Furner).
Measurement conditionsExecutive remarks on earnings calls. No control group5 or measurement method disclosed. Customers who already buy more may simply use Sparky more, so it can't be concluded that Sparky caused the extra spending.
LimitsA Walmart executive called it "still very early." About 85% of shopping still happens in stores. A large US retailer's own channel, so it doesn't transfer directly.

If we applied it

Most brands won't build an assistant like Sparky. Instead, they'll be the products that retailer, search and Meta AI assistants choose between. AI shopping assistants match a customer's goal against product attributes such as size, material, use and ingredients, and draw on reviews that describe specific use situations (agency analysis). If attributes are empty or the copy is a list of keywords, your product can't answer the situation the customer described.

Keyword-driven product copy (hypothetical · compact air purifier)Removes 99.9% of fine dust, low noise, smart IoT, high-efficiency filter, compact design
Rewritten from the customer's situation and intentFor studios and bedrooms (up to ○○ m²). Sleep mode runs at ○○ dB, quiet enough to leave on by the bed; the filter changes every ○ months without tools. Includes pre-filter cleaning guidance for homes with pets.

※ ○○ marks where actual product specs go. Never fill in unverified numbers.

The first version reads the same on any brand. The second answers the customer question "a quiet air purifier I can leave on all night in my small bedroom" directly. The same information also needs to go into product feed6 attributes (room size, noise, filter interval) for AI assistants to read it.

Reference case Zalando (European fashion platform) also found in 2023–2024 that customers using its AI assistant described situations, moods and budgets instead of product names, and shifted to recommendations reflecting location, weather and occasions (Zalando design team retrospective, Nov 2024).

For practitioners This week, pick one product and find the gaps in its product page and feed with five questions customers would actually ask (03 TRY THIS).

For clients To decide how to respond to AI shopping assistants, first check ① whether your main sales channels have AI assistants ② how complete your product attribute data is ③ whether reviews describing uses and situations are accumulating.

03 · TRY THIS

30-minute exercise: find the gaps in a product page with five customer questions

Goal
Check whether your product information answers the questions customers would ask an AI shopping assistant, and find the gaps
You'll need
Product page copy and feed attributes for one hero product, the official spec sheet, 10–20 customer inquiries and reviews with personal data removed, a generative AI chat tool
Time / level
About 30 minutes · beginner
Deliverable
A table rating each question "answered / partly answered / not answered" + a gap list + a one-page fix backed by evidence
  1. 5 min Write five questions using customers' own words from inquiries and reviews (e.g., "Is it quiet enough to run all night in a studio bedroom?")
  2. 10 min Use the prompt below to check whether the page and feed answer each question
  3. 10 min Check the results against the official spec sheet, remove inference and exaggeration, and refine the fix
  4. 5 min Enter the same questions into AI search and shopping tools and record how our and competitors' products appear
Goal: Check whether our product information answers the questions customers would ask an AI shopping assistant.

Input:
- Product name and target customer: [enter]
- Official spec sheet (verified facts): [enter]
- Current product page copy: [enter]
- Current product feed attributes: [enter]
- Five customer questions (based on wording from inquiries and reviews, with source numbers): [enter]

Write the output as a table with these columns:
Customer question | Answered / partly / not answered | Supporting copy or attribute | Missing information | Can the official spec sheet fill it (yes/no) | Draft fix

Rules:
1. Base each rating only on what actually appears in the copy and attributes provided.
2. Don't invent numbers, benefits or certifications not in the official spec sheet. If it can't be filled, mark it "needs confirmation."
3. Distinguish verified facts from your own inferences.
4. Keep the situational wording from the customer's question in the fix, but don't use superlatives (best, perfect, etc.).
5. At the end, list separately the feed attributes to add or fix.

Done when

All five questions have a rating linked to evidence, every number in the fix matches the official spec sheet, and the owner can pick one item to fix this week.

Practitioner deliverable A per-question rating table and a list of page and feed fixes

Client deliverable A one-page audit showing "customers ask AI this, and our product information currently answers this much"

Next step

After applying the fixes to the page and attributes, enter the same questions into AI search and shopping tools every 2–4 weeks and record changes in visibility. AI assistants' recommendations change often, so don't judge results from a single check.

A question for your team today

"When a customer asks AI about our product, what is our product page making it say?"

GLOSSARY

Glossary

  1. AI shopping assistant An AI in a store's app or site that answers questions, compares products and adds items to the cart for the customer. ↩
  2. Sponsored Prompts Suggested questions shown inside the assistant as ads, paid for by advertisers. ↩
  3. Average order value The average amount a customer spends per order. ↩
  4. GMV Gross merchandise value: the total value of goods sold through a channel over a period. ↩
  5. Control group A comparison group that didn't get the change. Without one, you can't tell whether the change itself caused the result. ↩
  6. Product feed A data file that organizes product information into set fields (title, price, stock, etc.) and sends it to shopping channels. ↩
