EDITOR'S NOTE

How much have we used the AI our customers use?

On Oct 11, Andrej Karpathy wrote on X: "The external world has seen the preview." Most people know AI as a free chatbot; only a tiny few have experienced the most advanced systems.

Marketers are no exception. Someone who stopped at asking a chatbot instead of searching, or getting draft copy, and someone who has handed an agent hours of work will draw different conclusions from the same news.

Meanwhile, customers are moving first. Their agents open our sites inside virtual computers, and their phones summarize the emails we send before they read them. Customer intent increasingly reaches us through AI. In our view, the only way to read that intent is to use the AI customers use ourselves.

You only see customer intent as clearly as you've used the AI your customers use.

※ This is the editorial view of this issue, not a proven fact.

01 · Key trend

How well people know AI has split into four tiers

What changed

Andrej Karpathy, an Anthropic3 researcher and OpenAI co-founder, wrote in an Oct 11 post on X that people's experience of AI narrows like a funnel, and that the gap is widening.

TierSize (Karpathy's estimate)The AI they see
Barely used itAbout 6 billionLittle contact with LLMs4
Casual free-tier users1–2 billion"A better Google search" or a writing aid
Professionals on frontier modelsAbout 20 millionMostly math and software experts, handling work that used to take weeks or months
Most advanced internal systemsAbout 5,000Thousands of agents collaborating for weeks on large software projects

※ The numbers are Karpathy's rough estimates, not survey results.

Evidence

Karpathy said AI is improving fastest in domains that are digital, verifiable and economically valuable — coding and math. He also warned that "human review and comprehension are starting to fall behind."

The same week offered a real example: on Oct 6, OpenAI posted 722 math manuscripts written with an unreleased model to GitHub5, and the next day withdrew three of them because of a single sign error. It laid bare the gap between how fast AI produces results and how fast people can check them.

Marketing impact

The gap exists inside teams too. In a Supermetrics survey released in March (435 brand and agency marketers in the US, UK, Germany, Australia and Singapore), 80% said they feel pressure to adopt AI, but only 6% had fully implemented AI in their work. Most of the pressure (89%) came from the C-suite and board.

If customers start shopping through agents while a team is still at the free-chatbot stage, it's hard to imagine what customers are actually doing.

Limits

Karpathy has worked at Anthropic since May, so his description of "internal systems" is a view from inside an AI company. The actual capabilities of those systems haven't been verified externally. Supermetrics sells marketing data tools, so its sample may overlap with its customer base.

Verdict

Do this week. Find out which tier each team member has reached, and book time for the team to try the AI shopping and search features your customers use, together.

02 · Agents

Agents running in VMs — giving AI a computer of its own

What changed

A chatbot only answers inside a chat window. Today's agents get an isolated virtual machine6 — a computer in the cloud — and inside it they open websites, create files and work for hours. Think of it as giving the AI its own office PC.

CompareChatbotVM agent
IsolationAnswers in text inside the chat windowDoes real work on a separate computer. Mistakes don't spread to your PC or other people's work
PersistenceEnds when the conversation endsWork state persists, so it can keep going for hours
PermissionsOutputs text onlyCan click, log in and even pay. What you allow is the key question
Evidence

On June 22, AWS released Lambda MicroVMs. It runs user- or AI-generated code in a separate microVM7 for each user or job, and can suspend and resume with state preserved for up to eight hours. It launched in five regions8, including Tokyo.

Meta's Muse, covered last issue, also runs inside a dedicated per-user virtual machine (Muse Secure VM).

Marketing impact

These agents are walking into stores on customers' behalf. On Sept 21, Amazon blocked Meta's Muse. Amazon said Muse came in without notice or permission, doesn't identify itself as an agent, and stores customers' login credentials and scrapes account data. Meta did not respond to press requests for comment.

Whether a visitor to your site is a person or an agent, and what to let agents do, has become a marketing question.

Limits

The AWS launch is a runtime for developers, and the announcement doesn't use the phrase "AI agents" (its stated uses include running AI-generated code and coding assistants). Amazon's claims are one side of the story, and Amazon is also a competitor building its own shopping assistant.

Verdict

Decide. Start by deciding whether outside AI agents may come into your site or store to browse and pay, and who makes that call. A practical checklist is in the Marketer Kit.

03 · Chips

AI moves onto chips — AI inside devices, and AI etched into silicon

What changed

"AI running on a chip" means two different things. One is on-device AI9, running inside phones and laptops; the other is the hardwired chip, with a single AI model etched into the circuitry. Neither is replacing the cloud so much as splitting the work with it.

BranchRecent newsWhat it means
On-device AIQualcomm said the NPU10 in its Snapdragon 8 Elite Gen 6, unveiled in September, can run models of up to 30 billion parameters on the device using a MoE11 architectureMore work handled directly on the phone, without a connection and without sending data out
Hardwired chipsCanadian startup Taalas unveiled HC1 in February, a chip with the entire Llama 3.1 8B model etched in. It claims about 17,000 tokens12 per second. Its next chip, HC2, targets models of about 20 billion parametersRuns only one fixed model, but fast and with less power
Industry movesAMD announced it will acquire Taalas on Aug 6 (terms undisclosed; closes after regulatory approval). It envisions GPUs13 reading the prompt and Taalas chips generating the answerNot "GPU vs. custom chip" but a hybrid of the two
Marketing impact

On-device AI already stands between customers and us. The iOS 26 Mail app shows summaries generated by Apple Intelligence by default, so customers can read the summary without opening the email. Link Tracking Protection14 strips tracking parameters from URLs in Safari, Mail and Messages.

The AI's summary appears in place of the preview text we wrote, and click-based measurement gets harder. Stating the key point clearly in an email's first paragraph matters more than ever.

Limits

Taalas's speed and power figures are all company claims and not independently verified. A hardwired chip can't change models, so a new model means a new chip. Qualcomm's 30 billion parameters assumes a MoE architecture that uses only part of the model at a time, and real-world speed varies by device.

Verdict

Check. Open a recent promotional email in the iPhone Mail app and see what the AI summary says, and make sure you're judging performance on clicks, purchases and replies rather than open rates.

04 · Platform updates

Operational changes to check this week

Platform update alert

  • Google Merchant Center15: Formatting guidance and a template for six "conversational attributes" that help AI understand products (question and answer, document link, related product, item group title, variant option, popularity rank) were expanded in September. They're optional and don't affect existing products' approval status. See the Marketer Kit for how to use them.
  • iOS 26 Mail and Messages: AI summaries are on by default, and Link Tracking Protection strips tracking parameters from URLs. If any reporting depends on link parameters such as ad click identifiers, check for gaps.
  • Amazon: Since Sept 21, Amazon has been blocking shopping by unauthorized AI agents, including Meta's Muse. If you plan to sell through agents, check each channel's policy first.

GLOSSARY

Glossary

  1. AI agent Unlike a chatbot that only answers questions, an AI that takes a goal, breaks it into steps, uses apps and actually carries out the work. ↩
  2. Parameters The numbers an AI model adjusts during training. More parameters usually means a bigger, more capable model that needs more computation. ↩
  3. Anthropic The US AI company that makes the Claude models. The AI used to produce this newsletter is also one of its models. ↩
  4. LLM (large language model) AI trained on vast amounts of text to understand and write like a person. The engine behind services like ChatGPT and Claude. ↩
  5. GitHub The world's largest site where developers post and share code. ↩
  6. Virtual machine (VM) A second computer built in software inside a real one. What happens inside it can be kept from spreading to the computer outside. ↩
  7. MicroVM A small virtual machine stripped down to start and stop very quickly. One is launched per user or job to keep them apart. ↩
  8. Region A geographic unit where a cloud company groups its servers, e.g., the Tokyo region. ↩
  9. On-device AI AI that runs directly on a device such as a phone or laptop, rather than on servers across the internet. ↩
  10. NPU A dedicated chip (or area of a chip) built to run AI calculations quickly and with little power. Found in recent phones and laptops. ↩
  11. MoE (mixture of experts) A design that splits a model into several "expert" parts and uses only some of them for each question. The whole model is large, but each answer takes less computation. ↩
  12. Token The unit AI uses to process text (roughly a word fragment). Tokens per second is how fast an AI writes its answer. ↩
  13. GPU Originally a graphics chip. Because it's good at doing many calculations at once, it's now the workhorse chip for training and running AI. ↩
  14. Link Tracking Protection An Apple feature that strips tracking tags from link addresses, making it harder to know who clicked where. ↩
  15. Google Merchant Center Google's tool for stores and brands to upload and manage product information (product feeds) for Google Search, Shopping and ads. ↩
