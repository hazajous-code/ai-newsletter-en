EDITOR'S NOTE

Into a search box we type words; into a chat we tell our story

In a search box, we type "running shoes." To a chatbot, we write: "I'm running my first 5K next month — what are some comfortable beginner shoes?" (hypothetical example). The example OpenAI gives advertisers shows exactly this difference.

Ad formats, bidding and image generation are features platforms open to everyone equally. Whether you know the customer's situation in the conversation — when, who and why they need the product — in the customer's own words differs from company to company.

The AI image rules that came out the same week point to the same place. What customers want to know is whether the item in the photo is what will actually arrive.

Can we write down, in the customer's own words, the situations customers confide to AI?

※ This is the editorial view of this issue, not a proven fact.

01 · Key trend

ChatGPT ads in 89 countries — attached to situations, not keywords

What changed

On Oct 21, OpenAI announced it had added 26 countries to ChatGPT ads, bringing the total to 89. It said that more than 100,000 advertisers have run campaigns in the little over eight months since ads launched. A pilot has been open in Korea since June 19.

PrivacyConversation content isn't shared with advertisers; only aggregate metrics such as views and clicks are provided

※ Based on reporting of OpenAI's Oct 21 announcement and coverage of the Korean launch (June 19). Country-specific terms for the 26 new countries were not disclosed.

Evidence

OpenAI's ads help page advises grouping ad groups not only by product line or service but by "customer need." The context hints inside each group are sentences that spell out "what is useful, to whom, and when." The help page's example replaces "running shoes" with "cushioned everyday running shoes for beginners training for their first 5K."

OpenAI says hints are not exact-match keywords7 or targeting rules, and don't guarantee placement in any particular conversation. It advises splitting groups when different uses need different messages or landing pages, giving the example of a plumber keeping "emergency repairs" and "routine maintenance" separate.

Marketing impact

According to CNBC, the annualized revenue8 of ChatGPT's ad business grew from $100 million in March to $1 billion in August. On a channel advertisers are flocking to, the unit of ad planning shifts from a "list of search terms" to a "list of situations customers are in."

Keyword tools don't produce that list of situations. It comes from material in which customers describe their circumstances in their own words: customer inquiries, reviews and support logs.

Limits

The 100,000 figure is a cumulative count of advertisers that have run campaigns; the number currently active hasn't been disclosed (The Information reported about 20,000 active advertisers at the end of August). OpenAI hasn't published performance metrics by industry, and the cost per click reported by early advertisers ranges widely, from under $3 to $10–13. Ads aren't shown to users on higher paid plans, so they may not reach the users who rely on ChatGPT most heavily for work (editorial judgment).

Verdict

Check now. Whether or not you run ads, check whether you can write down five "situation sentences" that customers actually used about one flagship product. If you can't, customer inquiry data comes before the ad channel. The exercise is in the Marketer Kit.

02 · Rules

AI-made product images mustn't pass as the real thing

What changed

On a single day, Oct 22, rules came from two places. Handmade marketplace Etsy changed its listing rules for sellers, and Pennsylvania's governor signed an AI ad disclosure law (SB 806, the Artificial Intelligence Deceptive Advertising Act).

ItemEtsy (announced Oct 22)Pennsylvania SB 806 (signed Oct 22)
ScopeItems listed on EtsyAds in which AI-generated or substantially altered images, video or audio mislead consumers about a product's nature, origin or authenticity
RequirementAt least one unedited photo of the actual item in a real setting. Instructional items such as patterns and recipes may not be AI-generated and must show a real finished result. No bot listings without human involvementA disclosure stating the ad "contains content modified or generated by AI," shown when the content first appears, in the same medium, and where possible hard to remove
ExceptionsAI-assisted images that accurately show the real item may be addedObvious fiction; parody, satire and commentary; text-only content
If violatedIf a buyer reports an item "not as described" and the image requirements weren't met, the seller may have to issue a refundFines of up to $3,000 and injunctions. Enforced only by the state attorney general and district attorneys
EffectiveDate not disclosed. Enforcement to be tested and expanded "in the coming months"60 days after signing (Dec 21, per law firm Loeb & Loeb's analysis)
Evidence

James Osman, Etsy's vice president of customer operations, called the change "a starting point, not a finish line." Etsy said it is also looking at ways to tell buyers whether AI was used. Pennsylvania's law dropped the draft's condition that it apply "only when knowing or reckless," so the test is whether it created a false impression, regardless of intent.

Korea has a background rule. Since June 1, Korea's Fair Trade Commission has enforced revised guidelines on endorsements and testimonials: when an AI-generated virtual person appears in an ad, it must be labeled a "virtual person" near the figure or at the start of the post.

Marketing impact

None of the three rules bans the use of AI itself. What they bar is misleading customers about the item they'll receive or the experience they'll believe in. Teams that make product images and model shots with AI should check against one test: "Would a customer who bought from this image be surprised when the real item arrives?"

Limits

The Pennsylvania law applies to ads seen in that state; Korean advertisers are directly affected only in campaigns targeting the US. Concrete standards for what counts as "misleading" will emerge only as enforcement cases appear. Etsy hasn't disclosed its effective date or how existing listings will be handled.

Verdict

Prepare. Make a list of product and model images created or substantially altered with AI, and mark for each whether a real photo accompanies it and whether a production record exists.

03 · Briefs

Also worth a look

  • Canva unveils its first in-house image model, "Craft" (Oct 22)A planning model first lays out the composition and any text to include, then a rendering model draws images of up to 2K. Canva said it was trained not on photos but on millions of Canva templates, and can place text in 55 languages. By Canva's count, it debuted fourth on the design comparison leaderboard9 DesignArena (the top three are all OpenAI models), and it's open to all users. Canva cited cost as the reason for its own model: "a few cents' difference per generation is a huge difference." In our view, image generation is becoming a standard feature of every design tool.
  • Anthropic's "Claude Duet," an agent that speaks up first (Oct 21)It checks connected apps every few hours to flag things to handle and sends a short briefing every morning. It works with its own browser in a cloud virtual machine10, and asks for approval before sending messages, paying, signing or deleting files. It's a waitlist-based research preview11 for US Pro and Max subscribers. It joins Meta's Muse and OpenAI's Dots in a race for "proactive" agents. If an agent scans a customer's inbox first and picks the "things to handle," our messages go through that filter too (editorial judgment).

04 · Platform updates

Operational changes to check this week

Platform update alert

  • ChatGPT ads: Expanded to 89 countries (Oct 21). In Korea, a pilot for adult users on the Free and Go plans has run since June 19. Buying is through technology partners or Ads Manager self-serve; country-specific minimum budgets and billing currencies weren't in the announcement.
  • Pennsylvania SB 806: Takes effect 60 days after signing. If you use AI-made product images, video or audio in campaigns targeting the US, check the disclosure wording requirements.
  • Etsy seller rules: One unedited photo of the real item per listing; no AI-generated patterns or recipes. If you sell on Etsy, review the photos in your existing listings.
  • Korea FTC virtual-person labeling (in force since June 1, background): If an AI virtual person appears in an ad, a "virtual person" label is required, and even with the label, presenting it as a real experience can count as unfair advertising.

GLOSSARY

Glossary

  1. Context hint In ChatGPT ads, a sentence in which the advertiser describes what kind of customer situation the ad fits. It takes the place of a keyword in search ads, but isn't a rule that requires those exact words to appear. ↩
  2. Image generation model AI that creates pictures, photos or designs from a text description. Many companies now offer models that do the same job. ↩
  3. AI agent Unlike a chatbot that only answers questions, an AI that takes a goal, breaks it into steps, uses apps and actually carries out the work. ↩
  4. Self-serve A setup in which advertisers create ads and set budgets themselves in a management dashboard, without going through a sales rep. ↩
  5. CPM and CPC Ways of pricing ads. With CPM you pay each time an ad is shown 1,000 times; with CPC you pay each time it's clicked. ↩
  6. Pixel and Conversions API Tools that tell an ad platform whether someone who saw an ad bought or signed up on your site. A pixel is code placed on a web page; a Conversions API sends the data directly from server to server. ↩
  7. Exact-match keyword A search ad setting that shows the ad only when a customer searches for the exact words you specified. ↩
  8. Annualized revenue (run rate) Revenue from a short period, such as the latest month, converted into a full-year figure by assuming it continues all year. ↩
  9. Leaderboard A ranking of AI models based on people voting between their outputs side by side. ↩
  10. Virtual machine (VM) A second computer built in software inside a real one. What happens inside it can be kept from spreading to the computer outside. ↩
  11. Research preview A trial version opened to some users before official launch to gauge their response. ↩
