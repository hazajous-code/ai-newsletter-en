01 · Case study

Royal Canin Czech Republic — the ad answered pet owners' questions first

Real caseVendor-published case (Google)Published Nov 2025 · republished May 2026Not independently verified

When Royal Canin launched search ads in the Czech Republic, it targeted the long questions pet owners actually ask instead of product-name keywords. The ad copy was tailored to answer those questions directly.

Company · timing · sourceRoyal Canin (pet food), Czech Republic. First published on Google's Think with Google US edition (Nov 2025), republished with the same figures in the India edition's "5 brands driving growth on the AI Search frontier" (May 27, 2026)
Problem to solveWhile preparing a new paid search campaign in the Czech Republic, reaching pet owners' long questions that the existing keyword approach didn't reach
Actual scopeSearch term matching and text customization in AI Max for Search5. A/B test run with an AI Max campaign
Customer intent revealedPublished example query: "how many times a day to feed a puppy." A question about how to care for the pet, with no product or brand name
How it works · human judgmentText customization adjusted the ad copy to answer the question directly. How copy was reviewed, how brand guidelines were applied, and whether landing pages changed were not disclosed
ResultsConversions up 263% at the same spend, CPA6 down 73%
Measurement conditionsA/B test. Control group setup, test period, the definition of a conversion (purchase or another action) and absolute conversion counts were not disclosed
LimitsA case chosen by Google, which sells the feature; not independently verified. Google states that "actual results vary by advertiser." Because this was a newly launched campaign, the possibility of a low baseline for comparison can't be ruled out (editors' judgment)

Reading questions as "jobs"

Someone asking "how many times a day should I feed a puppy" is checking how to care for the pet before choosing a food. Product-name keywords don't catch customers at this stage. If the ad answers that question first, the brand appears at a moment earlier than product comparison (editors' interpretation).

In the same Google material, Lufthansa's example query is "three-day weekend trip to Amsterdam including flights." Destination, length and conditions all fit in one sentence. It points the same way as Google's statement that AI Mode queries are on average three times longer than regular searches.

03 · TRY THIS

25-minute exercise: build an "intent map" from long questions in your search terms report

Goal
Group 20 long questions from the last 30 days of your search terms report by job, and produce draft ad copy answering the top 3 jobs plus a list of landing-page gaps
You'll need
A search terms report export (columns: search term, impressions, clicks, conversions), the official spec sheet for a flagship product, your current ad copy, and a generative AI chat tool. If you don't run search ads, use on-site search terms or customer inquiries instead
Time / level
About 25 minutes · Beginner
Output
Intent map table (job | representative question | number of questions | converted or not) + 3 draft ad copy lines + a list of landing-page gaps
  1. 5 min Sort the report by length, pick 20 queries of five or more words, and number them
  2. 7 min Group the questions by job with the prompt below, then read through and correct the groups so they match the actual wording of the questions
  3. 8 min Check the draft ad copy for the top 3 jobs against the official spec sheet, and delete any unsupported figures or benefits
  4. 5 min For each job, check whether the first screen of the current landing page has the answer, and make a "gap list"
Goal: Group the long questions in a search terms report by "what the customer is trying to do," and draft ad copy that answers the top jobs.

Input:
- Product / service: [enter]
- Official spec sheet (confirmed facts): [enter]
- Current ad copy: [enter]
- 20 long questions (numbered, with impressions, clicks and conversions): [enter]

Tasks:
1. For each question, write in one line what the customer is trying to do (learn, compare, calculate, solve a problem, buy, etc.) and quote the wording it's based on.
2. Divide the jobs into 3–5 groups, and list each group's question numbers, number of questions and total conversions.
3. Pick the top 3 groups by number of questions and conversions, and for each write a draft ad copy line of about 30 characters that answers the questions directly.
4. Mark whether each draft is supported by the official spec sheet, and compare whether the current ad copy answers those questions.

Output:
- Table: job | original representative question | question numbers | number of questions | total conversions | draft ad copy | spec sheet support (yes / needs checking) | current copy answers it (yes / no)
- At the end: questions the ad shouldn't answer (medical or legal judgments, definitive competitor comparisons, etc.) and why

Rules:
1. Don't invent figures, benefits or certifications that aren't in the official spec sheet; mark them "needs checking."
2. Prioritize the customer's actual wording over group names.
3. Flag groups that have conversions even if they have few questions.
4. Clearly separate confirmed facts from your own inferences.

Done when

All 20 questions fall into exactly one job group, the figures and conditions in the 3 draft ad copy lines match the official spec sheet, and for each job the landing page is marked "answered / gap."

Practitioner deliverable Intent map table + 3 draft ad copy lines + list of landing-page gaps + list of questions the ad won't answer

Advertiser deliverable A one-page report showing "the three questions customers ask most that our ads and pages don't currently answer"

Next step

Hand the gap list to the product-page FAQ and content planning. If you changed the copy, set a period to compare against the four weeks before the change, and rebuild the intent map the same way every month to flag newly emerging jobs.

A question for your team today

"If we read aloud the longest question that triggered our ad last month, does our current ad copy answer it?"

GLOSSARY

Glossary

  1. Search terms report A table in the ads dashboard showing which words customers actually searched when your ad appeared. ↩
  2. A/B test Splitting ads into two groups, changing only one side, and comparing results. It's used to pin down what made the difference. ↩
  3. Conversion When someone who saw an ad goes on to take an action the advertiser defined, such as a purchase, sign-up or inquiry. ↩
  4. AI Mode The conversational search view inside Google Search. It shows an AI-written answer first instead of a list of links, then takes follow-up questions. ↩
  5. AI Max for Search An AI feature added to Google search ads. It finds similar queries beyond your registered keywords to show ads, and adapts ad text to the query. ↩
  6. CPA (cost per acquisition) The ad spend it took to get one conversion. If conversions rise for the same money, CPA goes down. ↩
  7. Landing page The first page someone arrives at after clicking an ad. ↩
  8. Negative keyword An excluded term telling the system, 'Don't show my ad for searches containing this word.' ↩
  9. robots.txt A notice file in which a website tells the automated collection programs of search engines and AI what they may and may not take. ↩
