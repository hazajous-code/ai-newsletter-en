Customers ask about situations, not products

Material for turning this issue's weekly newsletter into work. See the case of a retailer that has fielded customer questions at scale, judge your situation with a checklist, then draft context hints1 for ChatGPT ads from customer inquiries in a 25-minute exercise.

01 · Case study

Lowe's Mylow — the customer "jobs" revealed by 25 million questions

Real caseMay and Aug 2026 earnings callsFigures: company statements · not independently verifiedQuestion types grouped by the editors

In March 2025, Lowe's launched Mylow, an AI advisor built with OpenAI. What customers asked Mylow about was less product names than the jobs they had to do at home and the conditions around them, and Lowe's connected those answers to product discovery.

Company · timing · sourceLowe's (US). Launch press release of March 5, 2025; Q1 earnings call in May 2026 and Q2 earnings call on Aug 19, 2026
Problem to solveChief Digital and Information Officer Seemantini Godbole: home improvement is inherently complex and can be overwhelming even for experienced DIY customers
Actual scopeStarted on the web (desktop and mobile) for loyalty members. Answers how-to questions based on Lowe's expert advice and finds related products. Adding a budget and ZIP code tailors recommendations to the area. A separate Mylow Companion is used by store associates
Customer intent revealedSample questions from the launch materials: When should I plant grass seed? / How do I measure for kitchen flooring? / How do I fix a leaky faucet? / How much mulch do I need? / Which washer-and-dryer pair saves the most on utility bills? / Which popular refrigerators can be delivered and installed tomorrow? The SVP of merchandising explained that "about 7 in 10 appliances are bought out of urgency (necessity)"
How it works · human judgmentConnects project advice to product discovery. Associates use Companion to help in-store customers. How answers are reviewed and how question data feeds into products and messaging have not been disclosed
ResultsAbout 25 million questions (customers and associates combined since launch, per Q2 call). More than 1 million customer inquiries a month (per Q1 call). Online customers who used Mylow converted at three times the rate of those who didn't (repeated on the Q1 and Q2 calls). In-store customer satisfaction up 2% when Companion was used (Q1)
Measurement conditionsThe comparison is "online customers who didn't use Mylow." Absolute conversion rates, measurement period and revenue contribution not disclosed
LimitsCustomers already ready to buy may be more likely to use Mylow, so the 3x can't all be credited to the tool (self-selection effect4; editorial judgment). Q1 comparable sales rose 0.6% year over year

Reading questions as "situations"

When the editors regroup the sample questions, three themes emerge: timing (when to plant grass seed), quantity and measurements (how much mulch, flooring measurements) and constraints and urgency (utility bills, next-day installation). None of the questions contains a brand or model name. What customers described was the situation in their own home, and the product comes up as the answer to that situation.

Context hints in ChatGPT ads call for the same raw material. You need sentences that describe "what is useful, to whom, and when" in terms of the customer's situation before you can split ad groups5 and write copy.

For practitioners If you collect "situation sentences" rather than product names from customer inquiries and reviews, you can use the same material for product-page FAQs and conversational ad hints (03 TRY THIS).

For advertisers Before considering conversational ads or an AI advisor, decide first: ① where customer question data accumulates ② who organizes those questions by situation ③ what you'll compare performance against, other than "customers who didn't use it."

03 · TRY THIS

25-minute exercise: pull "situation sentences" from customer inquiries and draft context hints

Goal
Pull situation sentences from real customer inquiries about one flagship product, and produce draft context hints and a plan for two situation-based ad groups
You'll need
The official spec sheet and current product description for one flagship product, about 30 customer inquiries and reviews with personal information removed (numbered), and a generative AI chat tool
Time / level
About 25 minutes · Beginner
Output
5 situation sentences (with source inquiry numbers) + 5 draft context hints + a plan for 2 ad groups + a list of "situations with no answer"
  1. 5 min Paste in the customer inquiries and reviews, and pull situation sentences with the prompt below
  2. 8 min Review the results grouped by timing, quantity, constraints and so on, and pick five situations that keep the wording of real inquiries
  3. 7 min Check every spec and condition in the draft hints against the spec sheet, and move situations the spec sheet can't answer to "situations with no answer"
  4. 5 min Split the five into two ad groups of different character, and confirm that each group's destination page answers that situation
Goal: Using customer inquiries, pull sentences describing "the situation the customer is in," and draft context hints for ChatGPT ads.

Input:
- Product and model name: [enter]
- Official spec sheet (confirmed facts): [enter]
- Current product description: [enter]
- Customer inquiries and reviews (personal info removed, numbered): [enter]

Tasks:
1. For each inquiry, write one sentence describing the customer's situation (when, who, where, and because of what constraint), with the source inquiry number. Leave out inquiries that only ask about a product name.
2. Sort the situations into 3–5 groups such as timing, quantity and measurements, space, user and constraints.
3. For each group, pick one representative situation and write a draft context hint. Format: "[who] looking for [product feature] for [purpose] in [situation]"
4. Mark whether each hint is supported by the official spec sheet, and whether the current product description answers that situation.

Output:
- Table: Group | Representative situation sentence | Source inquiry numbers | Draft hint | Spec sheet support (yes / needs checking) | Answered in description (yes / no)
- At the end: a proposal for splitting the hints into two ad groups of different character, with two lines on why

Rules:
1. Don't invent figures, benefits, certifications or compatibility details that aren't in the official spec sheet; mark them "needs checking."
2. Keep the customer's actual wording in the situation sentences.
3. Don't include claims that can't be used in ads, such as health or disease-treatment effects.
4. Clearly separate confirmed facts from your own inferences.

Done when

All five situation sentences link to real inquiry numbers, every spec in the draft hints matches the official spec sheet, and the two ad groups cover different situations.

Practitioner deliverable 5 situation sentences + draft context hints + a plan for 2 ad groups + a list of "situations with no answer"

Advertiser deliverable A one-page report showing "the situations customers are actually in that our product information and ads don't currently answer"

Next step

Hand the "situations with no answer" to the product-page FAQ and product-feed improvement lists. If you run conversational ads, use the hints and group plan as is, and every 2–4 weeks enter the same situation sentences into AI search such as ChatGPT and record how your product is presented in the answers.

A question for your team today

"Can we write down, in the customer's own words, three situations customers would confide to AI while looking for our product?"

GLOSSARY

Glossary

  1. Context hint In ChatGPT ads, a sentence in which the advertiser describes what kind of customer situation the ad fits. It takes the place of a keyword in search ads, but isn't a rule that requires those exact words to appear. ↩
  2. Earnings call A public meeting in which a listed company reports quarterly revenue and profit and executives answer investors' questions. The transcript becomes an official record. ↩
  3. Conversion rate The share of visitors or attempts that end in an actual purchase. ↩
  4. Self-selection effect When people who were already likely to buy choose to use a feature themselves, making the feature's effect look bigger than it really is. ↩
  5. Ad group A set of ads within a campaign grouped by the same theme or audience. Each set gets its own copy and destination page. ↩
  6. Landing page The first page someone arrives at after clicking an ad. ↩
  7. Product feed A data file that organizes product information into set fields (title, price, stock, etc.) and sends it to shopping channels. ↩
  8. UTM Tags added to the end of a link address that let analytics tools tell which ad or channel a visitor came through. ↩
