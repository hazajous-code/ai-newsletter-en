01 · Case study

How far are retailers opening the door to AI agents?

Real caseDisclosed in 2026Figures: company statements and press reports · not independently verified

As customers began telling AI agents "find something that fits these conditions and buy it," retailers had to decide whether to let those agents in and how much to hand over. As of early October, major US retailers had made very different choices.

ApproachCompanyWhat and why
BlockAmazonBlocked Meta's Muse on Sept 21, citing no notice or permission, no self-identification as an agent, and handling of customer login credentials. Building its own shopping assistant separately
OpenQVC · HSNLets agents complete purchases. Provides product catalogs6 to OpenAI and Google
Take overWalmartGemini surfaces Walmart products; when the shopper picks one, Walmart's shopping assistant Sparky takes over the cart. Walmart controls the rest of the journey
Open with conditionsTapestry (Coach, Kate Spade)Allows purchases in Google AI Mode7 and Gemini, but each order needs the shopper's explicit approval. No agent checkout on its own sites. Cites fraud and inventory hoarding
Stay closedKohl'sDoesn't accept purchases through agent channels

※ Based on PYMNTS reporting (10/5). Each company's policy may change.

Why Walmart pulled back from in-chat checkout

What it didFrom November 2025, sold about 200,000 products through OpenAI's Instant Checkout inside ChatGPT
ResultPurchases completed inside ChatGPT converted at about one-third the rate of shoppers who clicked through to Walmart's site (March 2026, Daniel Danker, Walmart EVP of product and design, as reported by WIRED)
What changedEmbedded Walmart's Sparky inside ChatGPT: shoppers log into Walmart, sync their cart, and pay in Walmart's own system. The same approach was applied to Gemini
Measurement conditionsAbsolute conversion rates and the comparison method weren't disclosed. OpenAI has since been moving away from in-chat checkout toward app-based checkout handled by merchants

Traffic through agents is still growing

On its Q2 earnings call in August, Shopify said AI-driven traffic and orders to merchants' stores had tripled year over year. The share of new customers among AI-channel orders was about twice that of other channels, and 75% of AI-attributed purchases came from outside the top 100 categories. Management also said the share of total GMV8 is still small.

In short, agents finding products is growing, while where checkout happens is still unsettled. Either way, the first thing an agent reads is your product feed and product details.

If we applied it — conversational attributes

At Google Marketing Live in May, Google announced "conversational attributes" for Merchant Center. They're optional attributes that help AI and agents understand products more precisely on AI search surfaces such as AI Mode, and adding them doesn't affect existing products' approval status. You don't need to repeat what's already in the product description.

AttributeWhat goes inExample (hypothetical · front-load washer)

For practitioners Turning the questions that come up most in customer inquiries into "question and answer" attributes adds to what AI reads without rewriting the product page (03 TRY THIS).

For clients To decide how to respond to agents, first check ① each main sales channel's agent policy ② where checkout should happen (inside the channel or on your own site) ③ whether your product feed contains the answers to customer questions.

03 · TRY THIS

30-minute exercise: draft "question and answer" attributes from customer inquiries

Goal
Pick the questions that come up most in real customer inquiries and draft them in the Merchant Center "question and answer" attribute format
You'll need
The official spec sheet and current description for one hero product, 20–30 customer inquiries and reviews with personal data removed, a generative AI chat tool
Time / level
About 30 minutes · beginner
Deliverable
Five question-and-answer pairs (formatted) + a list of "needs confirmation" questions the spec sheet can't answer
  1. 5 min Paste in the inquiries and reviews and use the prompt below to pull out the most frequent questions
  2. 10 min Pick five questions the official spec sheet can answer and draft the answers
  3. 10 min Check every number and condition against the spec sheet, and drop anything already in the description
  4. 5 min Format the pairs and ask your feed manager to add them via a supplemental data source
Goal: Draft Google Merchant Center "question and answer (question_and_answer)" attributes based on customer inquiries.

Input:
- Product name and model: [enter]
- Official spec sheet (verified facts): [enter]
- Current description: [enter]
- Customer inquiries and reviews (personal data removed, numbered): [enter]

Tasks:
1. Pull the 10 most frequent questions from the inquiries, in order of frequency, and note the inquiry numbers each is based on.
2. Of those, pick five that can be answered from the official spec sheet alone and write answers.
3. Mark any question already answered in the current description as "duplicate."

Output:
- Table: Question | Source inquiry numbers | Answerable from spec sheet | Duplicate of description | Draft answer
- Final line: the five chosen pairs in "Question":"Answer" format, joined with commas on one line

Rules:
1. Don't invent numbers, benefits, certifications or compatibility not in the official spec sheet; mark them "needs confirmation."
2. Keep the wording customers actually used in the questions.
3. Keep answers short and factual, with no superlatives (best, perfect, etc.).
4. Distinguish verified facts from your own inferences.

Done when

All five pairs link to real inquiry numbers, every number in the answers matches the official spec sheet, and none duplicate the description.

Practitioner deliverable Five formatted "question and answer" pairs and a list of "needs confirmation" questions

Client deliverable A one-page report showing "questions customers actually ask that our product information can't answer yet"

Next step

After adding them via a supplemental data source, enter the same questions into AI search such as Google AI Mode every 2–4 weeks and record changes in answers and visibility. Hand the "needs confirmation" questions to the product team as grounds for improving the spec sheet.

A question for your team today

"If a customer's AI agent came into our store, would we let it in? And if so, what would we want it to read on the way?"

GLOSSARY

Glossary

  1. AI agent Unlike a chatbot that only answers questions, an AI that takes a goal, breaks it into steps, uses apps and actually carries out the work. ↩
  2. Conversion rate The share of visitors or attempts that end in an actual purchase. ↩
  3. Product feed A data file that organizes product information into set fields (title, price, stock, etc.) and sends it to shopping channels. ↩
  4. Google Merchant Center Google's tool for stores and brands to upload and manage product information (product feeds) for Google Search, Shopping and ads. ↩
  5. Supplemental data source A Merchant Center option to upload only extra fields separately and attach them, leaving the existing product feed unchanged. ↩
  6. Product catalog List data of the products you sell, with names, prices, stock and specs. ↩
  7. Google AI Mode A search view inside Google where you ask and get answers conversationally with AI. ↩
  8. GMV Gross merchandise value: the total value of goods sold through a channel over a period. ↩
